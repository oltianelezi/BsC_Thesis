% Abstract (English)
\abstract
{
Football today is being evolving rapidly. This evolution is largely driven by implementation of performance analysis through technology, which the biggest clubs use to great effect. However, these methods are far too expensive for the vast majority of clubs and academies in the world, and are therefore inaccessible to them. This thesis addresses that issue by evaluating the current state-of-the-art technologies in object detection inside the football domain, and offering the best option for different practitioners spanning different budgets of consumer-grade hardware configurations. 

The three lightest variants of the YOLOv8 architecture are fine-tuned pre-trained weights on the Bundesliga Data Shootout dataset, a publicly available dataset which consists of annotated frames from broadcast footage from football games with annotations covering four different classes: player, ball, goalkeeper, referee. 

All three models are trained on identical conditions, and then evaluated on their accuracy and inference speed using standard metrics such as mAP@0.5 for accuracy and frames per second for inference speed. After a thorough analysis of the experiment and results is made, the findings are used to offer practical guidance for different practitioners and their goals, based on their hardware tier.
}
